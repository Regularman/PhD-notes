## Contribution

When DHS are used to support the operation of power grids, more frequent adjustments to the electricity generation/consumption of the heat producer are required. These fluctuations affect the DHS operation and are then propagated throughout the whole DHS.
- This may push the DHS on its operational limit (supply of reliable heat to the consumers or temperature limits of the pipeline networks.)

due to hydraulic lag, here will be delays in the propagation of thermal fluctuations, making flexibility calculations difficult.'

The main contributions of this paper is that
- Model based method to help DHS owners evaluate their maximum change of electricity generation/consumption for flexibility provision under the given operating condition
- Evaluation criteria for which DHS are suitable for providing flexibility services
- Key factors that affect the flexibility of the DHS

Examines, in this study, a DHS with a radial structure. There is only one heat producing unit (CHP that link the power grid to the heating network) in this study
## Content

Flexibility is described as the gap between actual generation by the system the demand which can be filled through either grid import or export.
- Different flexibility market differ in duration and ramp time (designing HPNs for different flexibility markets)

The ability of the heat producer to meet flexibility targets is affected by 
- lower and upper bound of the heat producer output
- heating network temperature, which should be kept within lower and upper limits.
- Building temperature lower and upper limits
- transport delays, where extra or shortage of heat will appear in another period.

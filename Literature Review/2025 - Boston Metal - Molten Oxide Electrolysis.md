## Pilot plant - 2018 to 2023

The end goal of the pilot plant is to demonstrate the stable production of iron and oxygen. It moved the TRL from lab scale testing of inert anode and pilot cell to long duration operation of the pilot cell.

1t per month scale facility in the pilot plant. 

- In the prior budget period, the plant produced a 10kg batch of liquid steel
- In 2023, the plant was able to produce a 100kg batch of liquid steel [Source](https://www1.eere.energy.gov/iedo/downloads/2023/peer_review/Boston%20Metal_Lambotte_IEDO_Iron-and-Steel_Carbon%20Free%20Iron%20for%20a%20Sustainable%20Future.pdf)
- In 2025, the plant was able to produce a 1t batch of liquid steel [Source](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/05/20/1137523/boston-metal-funding-critical-metals/)

The main development is a chemically inert anode design that can operate under the corrosive environment.

Currently looking to expand to a demonstration project that will be operational and commissioned by 2028.

## Commercial facility - Brazil

[Factsheet](https://www.bostonmetal.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Boston-Metal-Brasil-Fact-Sheet-EN-202607.pdf)

Aims to extract high value minerals from mining waste using MOE
- The company is focused on producing niobium, tantalum and tin ferroalloys
- This demonstrates the Molten Oxide Electrolysis cell at the industrial scale

Boston Metal's Brazilian high-value-metals facility, meant to produce niobium, tantalum and tin, suffered a refractory failure and electrolyte leak in January 2026, delaying startup to around September 2026 and triggering 71 layoffs in April 2026. A timeline of the Brazilian commercial facility is provided in [Source](https://www.alphaneo.ai/coverage/materials/boston-metal)

However, one of the differences is that the commercial iron making versus high value/rare mineral failure is more forgiving economically. Furthermore, the electrochemistry of niobium, tantalum and tin ferroalloys will be different to the production of liquid steel.
- Commercial steel also requires more uptime and scale compared to critical minerals

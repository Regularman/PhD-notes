[[2024 - Review of Business Models for Industrial Heat Pumps]]
## Contributions

Uses the business canvas model to study business cases for flexibility contractors, with a focus on the German market. The aim of the study is to help with economic analysis for the feasibility of heat pump feasibility.
- The study looks at the integration of thermal storage with a 5kWp PV plant (home residence) in Germany. Revenue will be provided through
	- Offering services to the grid: Revenues are generated through the provision of capacity and energy at the reserve market, reduction of grid fees if heat pump or thermal storage can be scheduled by TSO or DSO, and reduction of tariffs in certain contracts
	- Offering service to the balance group: Revenue made through operation of the heat pump with the spot market to optimise electricity prices, and demand management
	- Services to consumers through increasing self consumptions of heat pump and improved efficiency through forecasts and operational data
## Contents

- Useful or modelling revenue from the ancillary and reserve market
The stakeholders are the heat pump operators, balancing group authorities, and grid operators. Note that the balancing group operator is the entity responsible for ensuring that the scheduled load has the same as forecasted energy. Furthermore, the heat pump contractor will need technology providers, ICT providers, software companies, and energy traders to provide their value proposition, and system planners (D&C contractors).

The value proposition for heat pumps comes from 
- Reduced cost for heat
- Fixed additional revenue
- Reduced use of natural resources (confirmed through LCAs)
- Reduced CO2 emissions
- Reduced costs for grid operation

Note that there is also a certain size required or participation in the electricity market, as well as requiring licensing and certification.

These revenue opportunities are in tandem to the provision of heat to the consumers.
Furthermore, business models with high revenue potential and low complexity (PV self consumption and reduced grid fees) are already present in market.

Revenue potential in the balancing market lies in the regulations around forecast errors (there are some instances where forecast error is good if it aligns with the power market.) Scheduled generators have an obligation to reduce forecast error, and penalties are applied where there are forecast erros.

Aggregation can allow for participation in the pooling market without frequent changes in operation.

Heat pumps are more suited to shorter reserve periods, and therefore more suited to tertiary reserve markets which have a longer ramp rate (which required market policy and regulation changes). This could be an aim for regulators addressing policy changes (as well as the minimum capacity). 
## Gaps

Provides a fixed cost for electricity in heat pumps? (That is assuming a nominal operation hour?)
Economic modelling is fixed based rather than storage based. For example, they do not account for the capacity of the storage in spot market pricing.

- Only highlights how revenue is generated but does not talk about the type of contracts
- Costs for the business models have been neglected
- Does not look at the combination of revenue opportunities
- Did not perform simulation of revenues for more accurate results
- How does a change in operating condition affect the lifetime of the heat pump?

## Questions

What is the difference between primary, secondary, and tertiary reserve markets? The difference is the timescale at which each market bids at. Primary is the fastest and tertiary is the slowest.
- The reserve market is separate from the spot market (you can participate in the reserve market and then buy what you need from the spot market)

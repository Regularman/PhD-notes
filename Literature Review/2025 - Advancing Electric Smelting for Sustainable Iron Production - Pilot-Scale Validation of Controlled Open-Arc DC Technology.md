When replacing the blast furnace with an electric smelting furnace, the following criteria must be met...
- Productivity and reliability must match blast furnace
- Hot metal quality must remain the same (superheat for transportation and maintain chemical energy for BOF)
- Slag must be compatible with cementitious additive standards (must be low in iron oxide, which weakens the concrete and disrupts the curing process)
- Must be able to accommodate low carbon DRI feedstock, whether that be high/low carbon, briquettes, lumpy feed, or fines.

Open arc smelting technology provides the flexibility and control that is required to process DRI with different degrees of metallization, metal content, gangue compositions, particle size, and temperature.

## Smelting Technology

### AC vs DC smelting furnace

AC furnaces have three electrodes, while DC furnaces only have one electrode. DC arc furnaces typically perform better than AC furnaces, but may face difficulty in foamy slag control. 
- AC furnaces also have shorter arc lengths, lower arc stability, with less precise temperature control.
- AC furnace is more widely used in conventional steel production as it is cheaper and precise temperature control is not needed.
- DC furnaces have minor benefits such as lower electrode consumption and the ability to directly process iron fines
- However, in this case, DC smelting identified as more optimal for the electric smelting of DRI, due to arc stability, heat transfer efficiency and smelting intensity potential. Precise control is requried to achieve optimal metallurgical performance. 

There are two types of operations - arc smelting, and resistance heating through submerged electrodes.

When replacing the blast furnace with an electric smelting furnace, the following criteria must be met...
- Productivity and reliability must match blast furnace
- Hot metal quality must remain the same (superheat for transportation and maintain chemical energy for BOF)
- Slag must be compatible with cementitious additive standards (must be low in iron oxide, which weakens the concrete and disrupts the curing process)
- Must be able to accommodate low carbon DRI feedstock, whether that be high/low carbon, briquettes, lumpy feed, or fines.

Open arc smelting technology provides the flexibility and control that is required to process DRI with different degrees of metallization, metal content, gangue compositions, particle size, and temperature.

## Smelting Technology

### AC vs DC smelting furnace

AC furnaces have three electrodes, while DC furnaces only have one electrode. DC arc furnaces typically perform better than AC furnaces, but may face difficulty in foamy slag control. 
- AC furnaces also have shorter arc lengths, lower arc stability, with less precise temperature control.
- AC furnace is more widely used in conventional steel production as it is cheaper and precise temperature control is not needed.
- DC furnaces have minor benefits such as lower electrode consumption and the ability to directly process iron fines
- However, in this case, DC smelting identified as more optimal for the electric smelting of DRI, due to arc stability, heat transfer efficiency and smelting intensity potential. Precise control is requried to achieve optimal metallurgical performance. 

There are two types of operations - arc smelting, and resistance heating through submerged electrodes.

The DC SOAC (open arc furnace) with 150mm graphite electrode with am imbedded pin anode design, DC furnace. The voltage is maintained through a hoist system that controls the voltage at a specific target, which is made easier to manage as the system is DC.
## Pilot Project

Pilot project processes 10t of DRI

Achieved thermal efficiency of 424kW/m^2 of bath power, and a feed rate of 216 kg/h/m^2. The rate of energy loss was 96kWh/h.

This technology could support the lowe carbon pathways of DRI-EAF, which can integrate hydrogen DRI and remove the need for coke ovens and blas furnaces in the industry. The electric smelter is needed to remove impurities in lower grade iron ores before processing in EAF.

Confirmed stable and accurate arc control, evaluated metallurgical behaviour and tested feed distribution concept. The ability to balance the feed and power ratio is very important for open arc smelting processes. The pilot project went for 10 days. The main objective of the pilot project was initial assessment of arc and smelting behaviour, metal slag interaction, and process stability in a controlled, open arc environment. The plant operated continuously to emulate an industrial environment.

Ultimately achieved iron content of 88.5% and metallisation of 80% (the latter describes how much Fe2O3 has been turned into pure iron.

## Demonstration Project

Demonstration project processes 370t of DRI

Demonstrated scalability of the electric smelting furnc, process stability and benefits, confirmed high recovery efficiencies, and energy requirements. The trial went on for 34 days. The shell was much bigger than the pilot (at 3.4m instead of 1m. The objective of the demonstration was scalability, energy efficiency, and process consistency at higher throughputs.

Achieved through efficiency that is comparable to the pilot test. The energy loss was 350 kWh/h. Across different conditions, the smelting process was able to achive up to 99.7% Fe recovery. Overall test 2 ran for 699 hours and consumed 0.482 MWh of energy, while test 1 ran for 221.9 hours and consumed 31288 MWh of energy.

## Contributions

Aimed to look at the drivers of environmental impacts across district heating systems in $5$ district heating level solutions and $3$ standalone systems.

The study attempts to provide guidelines for accounting for said performance.
- Recognises district heating to have higher energy efficiency and capable of reducing primary energy use.

Highlights lack of literature that looks at the environmental performance of heating systems that looks at environmental impacts beyond climate change.

However, confined to space heating and domestic water supply using district heating and standalone supplies. The impact categories that were developed are 
- climate change
- photochemical ozone creation potential
- respiratory inorganics
- acidification
- eutrophication of freshwater
These impact categories were analysed in different European regions, from north, east, weat, and south Europe

#### Sensitivity analysis
LCA is built on a series of assumptions, therefore, the sensitivity analysis is important to understand if these assumptions impact the results significantly

- Efficiency: Efficiency drives district heating performance compared to natural gas use. Used 0.97 in the study. What happens if 0.9 was used for efficiency of gas condensing furnace?
- Further, there are different complement rates (natural gas infrastructure remaining) in renewable district heating solution. In the scenario where waste heat is recovered from industry, CC impacts more than doubled when the complement rate of natural gas increased from 20% to 80%.
- Neutrality of biogenic biomass carbon accounting: Upstream assumption of carbon neutrality. however does methodology account for the transportation and fossil fuel used in the harvesting of the biomass? Renewable Energy Directive in Europe considers biomass to be netural. Also does not consider the land use impacts.

## Limitations

The way that the network was designed is extremely generic
- Thermal density described as the ration of total delivered energy over the length of the network, using specified values of $2GWh/km$ and $10GWh/km$.
- While the installed capacity is calculated using a set diversity factor of $0.7-0.85$ depending on land use type, safety coefficient, and leak rate (efficiency of $98\%$ considered).

Geothermal system is based on the available data using a French system. This ignores the different geothermal potential of different areas.
- Dependent on water quality and soil temperature
- constrained by the lack of geographical data in Europe

Assume the same heat and electricity allocation, 54% of heat and $47\%$ of electricity from CHP units used in centralised units.

Seasonal rather than diurnal COP data was used.

Depth of heating pipeline is also a design factor $0.5$ to $3$m with a mean of $0.8m$. What are the preliminary and detailed design factors, what should we consider before we pass this research onto Iberdrola or other ESCOs.

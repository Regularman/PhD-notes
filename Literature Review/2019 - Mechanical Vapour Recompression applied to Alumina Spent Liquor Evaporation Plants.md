## Content

The paper explains the concept of boiling point elevation where the boiling point of the spent liquor is higher due to the concentration of the caustic solution. For example, the saturated vapor may have a temperature of $100\degree C$, but the boiling point of the liquor under atmospheric pressure is $106\degree C$, which means that the gas must be compressed to increase its temperature (this is known as dead or lost compression for the MVR system)

Compressors are also designed with integrated vapor scrubbers to capture liquor droplets being carried over. This scrubber is a curtain of water condensates that is sprayed counter current.

## Case Study

The case study is about how a MVR FFE evaporator is installed into an existing 5 effect FFE evaporation system
- 5 effect means that there are 5 falling effect evaporator in series, and the un-evaporated liquor (now at a higher concentration) is passed into the next evaporator in series.

The FFE was installed at the front of the train using two stage compression. 

The implementation of the MVR will reduce the operating cost in RMB from 24.5 RMB/t of evaporation to 20.6 RMB/t of steam. This ended up being cheaper than increasing the heat exchange surface area due to the steam re-use and energy efficiency.

Steam economy improved from 0.375t of live steam per tonne of alumina to 0.26t of live steam per tonne of alumina. The cooling water decreased from 14t/t to 11.8t/t. The concentration of the caustic solution remained the same for quality control purposes. ## Limitations

The study highlights that live steam is not needed. However, other case studies shows that full displacement of live steam may be limited by plant footprint and heat exchanger areas.

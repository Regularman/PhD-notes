## Contributions

The study looks at business models to drive the uptake of industrial heat pumps from the perspective of the heat pump manufacturer. This starts with a background review on the scientific literature and current landscape of heat pumps in the EU. This section usefully summarises all the project in the UK.

The author highlights that there are no literature on the business models of industrial heat pumps, and most of the available papers are on residential heating applications. The study conducted interviews with heat pump manufacturers through a questionaire. 
## Content

Suppliers of industrial heat pumps summarised in IEA Annex 58 projects. Note that there are 4 types of heat pumps, covering heat capacities of up to $10 MW$.
- standard heat pumps with supply temperatures $<80\degree$C
- high-temperature heat pumps with supply temperatures $>100\degree$C
- steam generating heat pump with mechanical vapor compression (MVR) and supply temperature $<200\degree$C
- heat transformer with supply temperatures $<300\degree$C

Provides many case studies of heat pump use with district heating in the references. Including [16], which showed in 2020, the optimal bidding strategy of heat pumps on the day-ahead spot market.

Currently, the sales of heat pump products with direct to customer focus is the most common business models with industrial heat pumps and leave customers with most of the risk, especially for newer technologies such as high temperature heat pumps. relevant to the heat as a service model are
- Leasing generates fixed income over the project duration
- Energy contracting, provided by an ESCO, guarantees performance in terms of efficiency. Revenue is additionally made from efficiency surpluses.
- Trading and arbitrage allows the ESCI to participate in the spot market, sell carbon certificates.
- HaaS, potential to reduce cost by recycling heat pumps. The company pays the upfront cost of installation, a fixed price per kWh of heat delivered, and maintainence cost.

Outlines that EaaS and HaaS business models are still unexplored according to heat pump manufacturer feedback. This is also due to high temperature heat pumps being of insufficient TRL in 2024.

There are different heat pump technologies, listed below, which requires their own business model evaluation,
- vapour compression, 
- MVR, 
- absorption, 
- thermochemical heat pump
## Identified Gaps

The paper essentially summarised industry opinion on the business models available, but does not comment on the suitability of HaaS and EaaS and how they may fit into the market. The PUSH2HEAT project will also include a PESTEL analysis.

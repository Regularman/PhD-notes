## Contributions

Presents the ESCO model and a review of the main barriers, they then present how to model risk and profitability of the model. Case study done of 26 housing associations in Finland, where they are still using oil burners and there are no ESCO that operates in the area.
- Porposes a business model for biomass based energy

Found that the ESCO model is unattractive in the current business climate (although this was in 2018!)
- This is due to hindering public procurement rule
	- There are high transactional costs, low level of information
- Low client confidence
	- Due to high complexity of these projects and relative low level of returns.
- Mismatch of financing
- unclear contracting terms

## Content

ESCO is split into 2 types of contracting 
- Energy supply contracting (Efficiency measures ends at the supply side)
- Energy performance contracting (final efficiency measures on the demand side)\

This can be further split into 
- Shared energy cost savings (shared across a fixed time period)
- Guaranteed energy cost savings model (assumes performance and credit risk)

This paper delves only into energy supply contracting (based on renewable wood energy supply). Energy shared saving option to simulate risk sharing between the client and the user. 

Behavioral economics highlights that discount rates are not constant but bullish in the short term and trends downwards over the long term. This creates a hyperbolic discount rate curve that represents that investors want higher returns in the short term and lower returns in the short term. 
- The expected total payment to the ESCO must include a risk premium in the final PV value.

The paper presents a model to calculate the profitability of the ESCO ownership, although this is through a simple PV calculation rather than considering the load profile and energy demands and trends in energy prices.
- The NPV is calculated based on the net revenue between the old system and new system, the subsidies involved.
	- This NPV is split between the ESCO and customer. This allows us to recalculate the necessary fees for ESCO payments. The study adjusts the risk premium value and see how they affect real data from the the Finish case study.

### Case Study

The case study just looks at consumption data for each housing association. However, it does not show how they chose the system sizing.

However, they assume
- No residual value, assumes that current systems requires no operational costs
- 10% subsidy for the wood pellets
- OPEX is 2% of CAPEX
- Price of pellet is assumed to be 53.4 euros/MWh constant
- Price of heating oil is 109.1 euros/MWh
- Interest rate is 5% and we assume 50/50 split

Case study showed that all options were attractive.

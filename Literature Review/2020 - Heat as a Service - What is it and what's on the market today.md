There are several category of risks for the ESCO

| Risk               |                                                                                                           |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Financial risk     | Late repayments for equipment financing                                                                   |
| Technical risk     | Maintaining the equipment for the expected lifetime                                                       |
| Energy price risks | You can take on the energy input price to provide the heat required                                       |
| Behaviour risk     | Changes to conditions (such as inefficient use), there has to be a way for the ESCO to mitigate this risk |
| Performance risk   | Taking on liquidate damages for guarantee of performance and efficiency.                                  |
| Regulatory risk    | Exposure to regulations as a novel market                                                                 |

## Comfort as a Service

Eneco is supplying true comfort as a service as a trial in 2020. We can look at how this trial went in retrospect.

Heat pumps are owned by Eneco. 
- Residents pay EPV fee to landlord, which is a fee offered in the Netherlands for providing an energy efficient property.
- Eneco also takes on energy risk as they supply electricity to the heat pump with wind power and there are also solar PV panels to provide power.

Residents can change the temperature provided that they don't use more than 5 GJ a day (adjusted annually)
- Residents also have the option of grid mix electricity as a backup contingency or to take advantage of low price events

Fully Comfort as a Service would also include lighting, cooling in the summer.

![[Screenshot 2026-06-20 at 11.34.50 am.png]]

There's also a value proposition for the installers, as they can focus less on selling the heat pump and more on installing, which is what they're good at.

**At the EOL, the customer typically owns the equipment for fixed term contracts. With indefinite model, the idea is that they are paying for the duration of the factory, and they are T&Cs about what happens if you are moving somewhere new, this then gives you the option to buy off the equipment in a factory**

Non contestable means that the network operator is selected directly by the infrastructure planner. In a contestable process, the network operator is selected through a competitive assessment process conducted by the Infrastructure Planner.

- This process is to ensure that an appropriate amount of the cost is passed through to the consumer. it follows the prudent, efficient, and reasonable yardsticks as specified by the AER. 

The difference is that in a non-contestable project, the AER determines reasonable, prudent and efficient costs through this methodology. Chapter 6A in the NER also deals with revenue determination and includes

- Regulatory asset base
- return on capital (AER rate of return instrument)
- depreciation
- Estimate cost of corporate income tax
- Forecasted opex
- forecasted capex
- Network support pass through
- Cost pass through
- Reopening of a revenue determination for capex

## How does AER assess revenue proposals from IPs?

#### What is included in a revenue proposal
Revenue accounts for the following components

- repayment of capex as determine in the Transmission Efficiency Test (prudent, efficient, and reasonable). staging also matters, but this would be too detailed for the level of analysis I'm trying to come up with. In addition to capex, the AER also assesses the augex, repex, non network capital expenditure, and connections and customer initiated works capital expenditure.
- Return on capital costs that have not been repaid
- An allowance for the operating costs
- indexation of the regulatory asset base (accounting for inflation). Justification will have to be made for the indexation rates
- Estimated cost of corporate income tax of the network operator
- An increase or decrease in network operator revenue due to the incentive schemes
- Compensation of risks identified by the network operating
#### Maximum capital costs
This includes the development and construction costs and acts as a maximum costs allowed by the consumer Trustee

## Depreciation

Depreciation schedules are prepared in [NER Chapter 6A 6.3][https://energy-rules.aemc.gov.au/ner/792/856534] or equivalently in EII clause 6A 6.3. The key work in this is determining a depreciation schedule that reflects the nature of the asset over its economic life.
- The asset depreciates to $0$ over the assigned asset life in a straight line manner offset by the predicted inflation. 

## Further readings

The AER also publishes the expenditure forecast assessment guideline. This is where they outline how they calculate the three areas of prudency, efficiency, and reasonability.
```
We consider that the notion of efficient costs complements the costs that a prudent operator would require to achieve the expenditure objectives. Prudent expenditure is that which reflects the best course of action, considering available alternatives. Efficient expenditure results in the lowest cost to consumers over the long term. That is, prudent and efficient expenditure reflects the lowest long-term cost to consumers for the most appropriate investment or activity required to achieve the expenditure objectives. 
```

Guidelines on expenditure incentives, efficiency benefits sharing, and capital expenditure sharing incentives are also published.

Electricity post tax revenue mode (PTRM) can be used to calculate the expected revenue for the network operators.

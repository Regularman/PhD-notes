394 m^2 of evacuated tube collectors used to supply heat to the coating process in radiator manufacturing, using water as a HTF to provide a boost to the boilers or set to 85 °C to prehgeat the boilers. Due to the smalle size of the bath, only a 5m^3 storage tank is installed.

## Gas pipeline heating - Enersolve DmbH

For the economic transportation of natural gas over long distances the gas needs to be under high pressure. Therefore, GPRS are implemented to reduce the pressure before being supplied to the end-consumer. By reducing the pressure of natural gas, the temperature is reduced due to the Joule-Thomson-Effect, which can cause freezing of the GPRS components and thus a safety risk. To avoid freezing, a remarkable amount of energy is needed to preheat the gas before its expansion

355 m^2 of flat plate collectors backed by a natural gas burner with water glycol mixture used as a heat transfer fluid. There is 25 m^3 of short term water storage with a volume of 25 m^3

It has a total solar fraction of 15% for the system, delivering 190 MWh pa. Assuming natural gas is used as the base case, this represents 0.038 ktCO2-eq pa of emissions savings.

## Mash tun heating - Goess Brewery

Note that the entirety of process heat demand can be supplied between 25-105 °C, in processes such as malt drying, bottle cleaning, pasteurisation, and mashing. In this project, the mash tun was retrofitted with new heat exchangers to be heated with hot water instead of steam. Stagnation, where steam production exceeds demand, can be addressed by pumping hot water from the storage to cool the collectors and an additional water/water heat exchanger can cool down the collector field. 

1500 m^2 of flat plate collectors with 200 m^3 of stratified storage, also include s a vapor condenser to recover the waste steam. Uses a water glycol mixture as a HTF. Includes non-return valves to prevent convection losses from the collectors and at night.

In sum, the implementation of this technology saves 1570 MWh-th of natural gas consumption per year, which corresponds with 0.319 ktCO2-eq pa. Note that the data is from simulation, operational data is unclear.

The solar gains are 280 kWh/m^2 annually, with lower efficiencies because 85°C represents the upper limit at which flat plate solar thermal technology can supply.

1365 m^2 of evacuated tube collectors, and the process diagram shows that a separate heat exchange/ST system is installed for each tank.

## Contribution

Proposes the installation of solar thermal panels at district heating substations, looking at the impacts of reducing the cost of domestic hot water production for the end user, and reducing impact of energy production on the environment.

Used TRNSYS to model the system, including specific equipment and climatic parameters of Bucharest.
## Content

Solar thermal panels, in combination with PV panels, are three times cheaper than hybrid PVT panels (These contain vacuum tubes for heating and PV silicon cells) for conversion of solar energy into electricity.

![[Screenshot 2026-07-03 145751.png]]

The solar thermal panel is directly connected to the storage tank. Cold water from the network is sent to the consuming district heating substation for use in hot water applications.
- Type used was a standard, flat, solar thermal collector model
- Float vessel for expansion and the water storage tanks is a spiral heat exchanger type 534
- The storage tank is a Viessman Vitocell 340-M

Note that wind is an external factor that influences the efficiency of solar panels, as it cools and ventilate the collecting surface of the solar radiator.

## Limitations

This study just sizes the solar thermal panels and does not show the impact of integrating solar thermal panels in existing systems, or compare it with the business as usual case.
- can look further into how secure the implementation of solar thermal is to meet the hot water demand
- Does not comment on emission savings or project cost of integration

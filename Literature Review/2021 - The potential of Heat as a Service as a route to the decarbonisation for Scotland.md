The UK has distributed $600M pounds since 2014 on the domestic Renewable Heat Incentive payments to gap the price difference between fossil fuel and low carbon heating systems.

**Support to buy a new heating system:** Companies such as EDF (FutureHeat) and Shell Energy (B-Snug) are covering the cost of heating system and control, with an annual subscription fee over X years to pay off the item. Alternatively, there are also Pay as You Save schemes, however, this is not as relevant to industry applications.
- Companies can rely on government subsidies to help with these payment

**Paying for heat**: Either through flat rate or unit price of heat. For example, Powergen offered homeowners a fixed monthly price for their gas depending on the size of their homes to increase price certainty. There can also be a subscription based contract (such as with Thermondo), where the monthly prices includes renting the heating system and all servicing, as well as the amount of heat used.

**Paying for comfort:** Energiesprong, originating in the Netherlands, is a model that is based on a provider such as ENGIE entering a long term contract to carry out a deep retrofit of the households (including new insulation, low carbon heating, and full system replacements and control). The household will continue to pay for the energy they use with their supplier
- The ESC funded by the UK government funded Smart Systems and Heat programme, developed heat plans that use warm hours rather than kWh. This was implemented in 100 homes in a living lab across england. Consumers buy warm hours rather than kWh, providing price certainty to the consumer.

### Provided case studies

All companies and case studies focus on social housing first due to modifiable housing stock and control over asset maintenance and ability to lock into longer service contracts.
#### Danish Energy Agency heat pump support scheme

$2.4M$ euro was invested to help $4$ companies towards the cost of delivering the scheme over $4$ years to 350 consumers. 
- Customers pay an up-front fee for the installation of heat pumps, and a fixed price per MWh of heat (rather than electricity), and a fixed annual payment to the service provider. Minimum contract length of $10$ years.

As a result of the scheme, more heat pumps have been installed, adding another option to just buying or leasing.
- Companies highlighted that subsidies are required for market entry.
- Consumers also like the convenience of the scheme in only working with one third party provider

In addition to this scheme, regulations through building codes can also help low carbon heating up takes. The DEA has a platform of a list of heat pumps with all their properties, tested by third parties.

- However, Denmark also has a history of district heating and there is a stronger public awareness of heat pumps.
- Some homes may also need upgrades before they are comfortable with heat pumps
#### Connected Response - Heat with Rent model

Heat with rent model has a risk surrounding residents struggling to stay comfortable for a reasonable energy cost. Connected Response helps by using smart control to charge during optimal time periods to make this model more commercially viable.

#### Energiesprong - deep retrofit

Managed Energy Service Agreement model where the provider typically takes on the energy supply contract of the end customer.
- MESA contract favors longer term contract for revenue certainty after installing energy efficiency improvements such as external wall installation, low carbon heat, electricity generation and storage. This is better for charging individually based on energy use, as it punishes people who are not able to make the investment for energy efficiency measures. For example, energy bills may be different due to how much sun an apartment gets, and therefore different tariffs must be applied.
- Margin due to energy efficiency creates commercial viability.

The model provides guaranteed comfort outcomes (21$\degree$C) for the consumers. The deep retrofit first approach ensures certainty and confidence in the comfort levels.
### Tariff Structure

Energy supply license requires suppliers to bill for units of energy used by customers, which limits ability to impose more creative tariff structures.
- Risk in flat rate tariffs, unlimited energy tariffs, and heat with rent is that the cost to serve each consumer is based on simple assumptions. This can be fixed through smart metering and energy modelling tools to de-risk for HaaS contractors.

## Contributions

Recognised gap in the application of hydrogen heating in industry in the UK, as most projects are focused on residential heating. The paper aims to propose the integration of hydrogen with existing solutions as well as waste heat recovery systems. The Organic Rankine Cycle (ORC) is used to recover power from waste heat. 

Conducted an industrial waste heat assessment to quantify how much waste heat is available, as well as a review of literature on waste heat potential in different industries.
## Content

Provides a method of calculating the LCOH of High Temperature Heat Pumps.

![[Screenshot 2026-06-04 112857.png]]

The paper also have many benchmarks and references to benchmarks for the relevant technologies of heat pumps!

However, they found that the contribution of hydrogen generated through the WH to electrolyser pathway is only meaningful when supplemented by external $H_2$ sources.

Used Sankey diagrams and tornado plots to illustrate sensitivities of different parameters. Ultimately found that waste heat potential and mean WH temperature is important to providing industrial heat. 

Compared the LCOH for different technology types. Although HTHP are the cheapest, waste heat resources are finite and not scalable to meet all industrial heat demand. Hydrogen is suitable for industries that requires high temperatures $> 400\degree$C, such as cement, aluminum, and metal sectors. 

Hydrogen is a good transition fuel as it can be blended with natural gas in boilers before full adoption of green hydrogen.

Final LCOH of $\$85 euro/MWh$.

One of the main challenges is the supply of electrolysers, given current market demands, which has a lead time of $3-4$ years.

## Content

Questions the assumption that district heating is an optimal solutions, offering an analysis of how to best deliver heat to consumer from a first principles approach.

Conducts a cradle to grave analysis of 3 different heating systems for a residential neighbourhood of 1000 inhabitants in Tuscany, Italy, serving sanitary hot water, space heating and cooling
- Geothermal heat pump (centralised)
- Biomass system (centralised)
- A district heating system using natural gas (centralised, benchmark)
- A system with boilers and air to air heat pumps, diffused at each dwelling (benchmark)

Functional unit is 3250 MWh/yr which is the energy consumption of the neighbourhood.

The data refers to 2007-2011 period/ Cradle to grave analysis

Looks at technology and standalone vs distributed applications.

## Limitations

- Does not vary the load and therefore represents a static analysis of the system
- Heat pump for the geothermal scenario are statically sized at 41.5MW and has a constant COP value of 4.2
- The boundary of the system cuts off at recycling and only includes assembly, operation, and EOL)
- Does not include the transportation of natural gas or other resources, nor the extraction of mineral required for heat pumps and geothermal systems

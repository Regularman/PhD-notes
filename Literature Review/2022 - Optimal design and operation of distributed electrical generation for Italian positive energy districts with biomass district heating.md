## Contributions

Directive 2019/944 in Italy proposes the development of Positive Energy Networks, which are self sufficient, net positive balance energy communities. In this scenario, a district heating biomass powered network is integrated into a P2P distributed energy network, as well as connected into the wider grid.

![[Screenshot 2026-09-26 at 8.26.07 pm.png|414]]

In this study, the district heating network has a centralised biomass boiler, with booster heat pumps and auxilliary gas boilers to meet demand if there is a production shortfall.
- The study varies to the threshold of P2P connection distance to investigate its impacts on the PED's self sufficiency. However, this mode so applies to the electricity generated from localised PV modules. Non-linearity comes from the demand of each building and their production capacity, based upon orientation and available roofspace (which is why GIS is important)
	- Scenario #1: $100m$ 
	- Scenario #2: $150m$
	- Scenario #3: $200m$ 
## Results

Demonstrates that increasing connection radius for each building increases self sufficiency and reduces carbon emissions due to the reduction in use of fossil fuel

Note that the study is quasi-dynamic, meaning that it considers load profiles between the different buildings.

## Limitations

Note that the study does not look at the economic aspect of increasing distribution network transmission length, nor its impact on heat losses and energy efficiency.
- What is the business model that will allow cost parity in this distribution network, who will own it?

Furthermore, the study does not quantify the impacts of electrification on the grid, although the maximisation of self sufficiency does reduce stress on the network and constitutes as a non-network solution.

## Contribution

Highlights that cost optimal solutions are restrictive, and an increased willingness to pay can lead to energy systems that are more resilient to sensitivities and varied operating conditions. Through the application of SPORES modelling, Lombardi is able to the technologies that are must haves (PV) or hard to replace (bioenergy for instance).

Spatial considerations comes from location of demands and its relative position to the deployed renewable generation.
- For example, Lombardi highlights that the regions which have a high degree of adjacency to other zones was able to achieve higher utilisation of renewable energy as different zones have different weather conditions, and thus they were less susceptible to poor renewable output due to weather.
Other mechanistic reasons for why locational variables matter includes 
- variability of renewable generation as a function of the siting of wind and solar capacity
- The interdependency of such siting and the resulting distance from the point of consumption
These locational considerations gave rise to two primary strategies for the Italian energy system
- coupled solar and battery for local generation'
- wind and large transmission/gas pipeline investment to move energy around the country

In addition to cost, the SPORE solutions are compared based on their 
- concentration of wind generation in a region
- transmission overcapacity
- renewable energy overcapacity

The SPORES method ensures ~={green}maximally=~ different from the cost optimal solution, within a $5,10,20\%$ cost margin of the cost optimal case.

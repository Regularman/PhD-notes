## Content 

In addition to IoT enabling a better value proposition for manufacturing companies, there is also the option of digital servitisation, in which digital services are offered to end consumers

Every value activity is split into a physical activity and an information gathering component.
- As businesses drive down their cost, they are moving towards digital components 

IoT is a subset of Industry 4.0, as IoT based solutions are often applied to industries and smart industries are discussed in the context of Industry 4.0. The broader goals of Industry 4.0 is also achieved through integrating IoT with cyber-physical systems.

Note that IoT is not just about a network connection of computers, rather it requires
- Simplistic end devices for efficient data transfer (sensor interfaces, processing units, transceiver units, and power supply)
- Edge computing to reduce computational burden (nodes can communicate with each other through a WSN communication stack)
- Cyber infrastructure to perform analytic services and secure data aggregation
- Simplistic messages
The network consists of end devices, propagator code, and integrator function for analysis and control. This needs to be managed through hardware (sensors and embedded technologies), middleware (storage and computing tools), and presentation

The challenges of operating IoT is 
- energy conservation and usage
- organizing large scale networks
- security
- effective and prudent communication systems. Information must flow between people, between things and people, and between things.

Interestingly, it is shown that large firms and institutions find it harder to achieve the expected returns, as they are experience larger revenue but lower profits due to higher labor costs, working capital, and net assets (taking on the net asset risk leads to lower profits?).

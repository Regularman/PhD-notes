## Contribution

Utilising atomised and ionised hydrogen as reducing agents. It is operated in a gas-tight direct-current electric arc furnace with a hollow graphite cathode for material and gas feeding.

Carbon is only introduced in the process through the wear of the graphite cathode.

Currently in the TRL of 5. 

There is a pilot plant with a capacity of 100kg iron ore per trial in Voestalpine, Austria.
- This study looks at that pilot study and assess the impact of different refractory materials for their wear characteristic and input material of varying gangue level will affect the reduction process.

## Content

The practise of hydrogen plasma smelting reduction is a high temperature process that uses hydrogen and plasma energy to melt and reduce iron ores.
- The high reactivity of excited, atomics, and ionises hydrogen can reduce iron oxides efficiently

Iron ore is preheated (in the future, this can be done with hydrogen waste gas). The hollow tube C is where the hydrogen entrained with the iron pellets and burnt lime or silica comes into the reactor. 
- The anode is F and is located at the bottom of the crucible.
- N is the offgas port
- H is where the plasma is produced
- Only the lower crucible is lined with refractory material, the cathode and anodes are water cooled. Note that in the crucible, a natural gas heater is used to preheat the refractory to prevent the thermal shock (however, it is not clarified how much gas is used per batch)

The reaction vessel operates at 150mbar, prevents oxygen ingress and combustion inside the reaction vessel.
- The electrical circuit is closed via the steel shell which has ground potential
- When active, the cathode is moved up and down by a lift mechanism to the set target voltage. The maximum supply by the transformer is 360V and DC current of 2200A. The operation of the plasma torch is limited to 350kW.
- Nitrogen is used for increased melting rate in the pre-melting stage, hydrogen is used as a reducing agent, and argon for the stabilisation of the plasma torch.
- It is not possible to tap the melt, and the reactor must be dismantled to separate into slag and metal phases.

During the preheating period, the charge might not be added to prevent the reoxidation of the pre-reduced material (DRI pellets or slag materials).
The cathode is lowered until it comes into contact with the charged material, where the arc will ignite directly at contact. When a slight melt pool has formed and the target current is reached, the cathode is raised and the nitrogen is mixed to the plasma gas for increased heating rate.
- The voltage need to be moderated so it does not exceed the transformer supply voltage (or the arc will distinguish)

Note that the slag and metal phase will separate when the crucible is allowed to cool down in an inert nitrogen atmosphere.

### Limitations

Note that the process has not been optimised yet, and the pilot plant was primarily used to figure out the reduction kinetics of the experimental setup,
- The cathode will have to be redesigned for a larger throughput and facilitate large power input
- The bath's surface area to volume influences reduction significantly

## Contribution

Shows that 100% of the steam supply can potentially be met via internal recovery
- 43% from calcination
- 9% from digestion
- 37% from evaporation
- 11% from precipitation

This represents a 79% reduction in energy consumption using MVR to recapture the steam. Results are only theoretical results simulated in Aspen Plus.
- Proposes that you can recover 0.6-0.8 ton of steam per ton of $Al_2O_3$, which equates to $1.4-1.8 GJ/t_{AL_2O_3}$, which is greater than the steam demand in the Bayer circuit.
- However, there are high cost barriers to implementation. Therefore, this study provides a techno-economic analysis of MVR, where high capital cost and OPEX that is sensitive to electricity prices is preventing widespread adoption.
	- CAPEX has a $\pm 30\%$ that were obtained in collaboration with Worley

This has the potential to remove 70% of carbon emissions from industry.

## Content 

The study shows that hydrogen oxy-steam calcination is different from fully electrified calcination. This is as the steam has a high concentration compared to using natural gas air combustion, which is diluted with nitrogen.

The paper also investigates novel configuration to remove fine particles in the waste steam after calcination process.

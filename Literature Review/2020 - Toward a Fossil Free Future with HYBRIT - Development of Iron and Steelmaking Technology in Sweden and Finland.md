### Iron pellet production

Fossil fuel free pelletisation developed at LKAB's pellet plant in Sweden during 2020-2021.
- Uses bio-oil to switch to 100% renewable fuel for their straight grate plant
- This is difficult because bio-oil differs from that of fossil fuel and bio-oil is more corrosive. This required new pipelines and storage tanks.
- There also needs to be electric heating and pumping of the bio-oil
- They are also testing carbon free fuel such as hydrogen and plasma.

Magnetite is mined locally
- It is first dried after being mined
- Crushed and sorted into granules of <15cm, separating out waste rocks through a magnetic separated
- The ore is then crushed into even smaller fractions into iron ore fines
- In the concentration plant, the ore is milled so that impurities can be removed and the iron content raised blending with water to form a slurry
- Pellets are mixed with limestone and dolomite or olivine
- The slurry is then passed into a furnace, heated by fossil fuel free fuel and blended with a binder called bentonite in large rotating drums to form diameters of around 1cm.
- Heat treatment (induration) is needed to improve the strength of the pellets, and in this process a large amount of energy is freed when the magnetite oxidises to hematite, reducing energy requirements by 60%.
- Finally, pellets are sintered at $1300\degree C$
- They are then cooled and transported into silos.

### Direct reduction of iron ore pellets

Trials ran from 2020-2024. Pilot facility can produce 1tph.

1. Iron pellets have to be sieved so that pellets are uniform size. This ensures even gas flow of reducing gas around the pellets
2. Sponge iron is either stored as small balls or pressed into briquettes to prevents re-oxidisation of the directly reduced iron
3. They are then taken into the EAF, cold, made into steel.

Green hydrogen is made on-site. The hydrogen is heated up to around $1000\degree$ before being used as a reducing gas. The water vapor is recycled as raw material for green hydrogen electrolysis and excess hydrogen is recycled and reheated.

- Hydrogen is stored underground (they also have another CCS pilot project)

### Electric arc furnace

The EAF is in Swerim, and has the capacity to produce 3-4tph of steel. It has a power of 5MW and 3 graphite electrodes (AC EAF)
- This will be 1/20th the size of the HYBRIT demo furnace

The furnace gas is sent via water and air cooled ducts to the gas cleaning plant, where particulate matter is separated from the off-gas at $8000m^3ph$.

Smelting can be done in a batch or semi continuously. Can take scrap, DRI, and HBI.
- A hopper above the EAF charges the EAF with dolomite/lime (slag former) and DRI/HBI

The steel normally maintains a temperature of $1550\degree C$. Oxygen is continuously introduced which provides energy and contributes to slag forming.
- Carbon is added using using bio-carbon
- Foaming is important to prevent damage to the refractory lining and excessive electricity consumption
- During smelting process, it is important to maintain a balance between feed rate and power input to prevent the formation of unmolten lumps of DRI/HBI called ferrobergs.
- In the final stage of charging, the temperature is raised to $1680\degree C$ by reducing the feed rate and the steel is sent for ladle treatment and casting

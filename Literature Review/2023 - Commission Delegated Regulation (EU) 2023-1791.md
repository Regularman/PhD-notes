Under Annex X Part III, the economic potential for different technologies used for heating and cooling shall be carried out using the cost benefit analysis outlined in Article 25(3). This identifies opportunities for more efficient heating and cooling technologies, distinguishing between energy derived from fossil fuel and renewable resources where applicable.

Promotes an energy efficiency first principle! This directive mandates that each EU country develop and publish their own CBA methodology in accordance with this standard.

The methodology is applicable for 
- Industrial waste heat and cold
- Waste incineration
- High efficiency cogeneration
- Renewable energy sources such as geothermal, solar thermal and biomass (other than those used for high efficiency cogeneation)
- Heat pumps
- Reducing heat and cold losses from existing district networks
- district heating and cooling

Net Present value is used as a basis for both economic and financial analyses. Projects with a positive cost benefit outcome are those where the sum of discounted benefits in the economic and financial analysis exceed the sum of discounted cost

## Base case

Baseline scenarios take into account existing policies at the time of compiling this comprehensive assessment (cut off is the end of the year preceding to the year by the end of which the comprehensive assessment is done)

## Alternative scenarios 

Alternative scenarios to the baseline shall take into account energy efficiency an renewable energy objectives of 2018/1999, taking into account

- Economic potential of technologies examined using NPV as criterion
- GHG emissions
- Primary energy savings in GWh per year
- Impact on the share of renewable in the national energy mix

# CAPEX breakdown

Costs:
- Capital cost of plants and equipment
- Capital costs of the associated energy network (Kind of considers the grid operator but not to the full extent of energy security)
- Variable and fixed operating costs
- Energy costs
- Environmental, health and safety costs
- Labour market costs, energy security and competitiveness, to the extent possible (energy security costs is considered, but how to implement?)

Benefits:
- Value of output to the consumer (heating, cooling and electricity)
- External benefits such as environmental, GHG emissions and health and safety benefits
- Labour market effect, energy security and competitiveness, to the extent possible

The approach needs to include heating and cooling demand trends and characteristics. Assumptions involved are
- Input and output prices
- discount rate
- energy price development in each regional context
- The system boundary must include the planned installation and the heating and cooling load (The total cost of providing heat and power shall be determined for both cases and compared)
- fuel type
- planned usage and the number of planned operating hours every year and and electricity and thermal demand
- Location

Sensitivity analysis to different energy prices, levels of demands, and discount rates must be included.

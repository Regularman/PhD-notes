## Contribution

We present a novel methodology that identifies and ranks combinations of optimal technology for industrial heat production at low temperature (100–150 °C) using a genetic algorithm alongside three distinct sustainability-based ranking approaches.

 Results show that while electrification through mechanical heat pumps performs well under conventional life cycle weighting (R1), more stringent sustainability-oriented rankings (R2, R3) tend to favour hybrid solutions that combine electrification with gas or biomass boilers. Notably, no configuration fully meets all sustainability thresholds, with resource use often exceeding sustainable limits ‒ even in the most favourable scenarios.
 
The functional unit is defined as the production of heat required to meet the hourly demand of the industrial processes described in Section 2.1.1, over a one-year period, in each of the five selected representative countries (see Section 2.4.1) and for three future time horizons: 2015–2040, 2040–2065, and 2065–2090.
- Furthermore, considered R1, which is the weighting method given by the LCA EF3.o method. R2 and R3 imposes stricter limits on the transgression of any one category
- C1 through to C5 looks at different effects of the electricity system (different generation technology)

#### Indicators used

Impact categories, classification, and characterization are done using the EF 30 LCIA methodology recommended by the EU JRC 
- Also included Cumulative Energy Demand and Cumulative Exergy Demand as performance indicators
- Sustainability limits are assigned using EVA methodologies

## Content and Results

The study highlighted that extensive deployment of heat pumps will exceed allocated sustainability limits in the resource use indicator for fossil, minerals, and metals by up to a factor of $9$.

Fruit juice is produced from evaporating water (12-70%) from fruit solids. The evaporated water contains the aroma solids that are condensed into a suitable aroma unit. Multiple MVR stages are required to reach the final concentration. Note that freeze concentration and reverse osmosis does not achieve the required end concentration and are therefore not seen in this evaporation process.

- With all MVR technologies, we are essentially shifting steam to electricity requirements. ~={red}What is the displacement factor?=~

This evaporative process is very similar to the flash train in the alumina evaporation process in the Bayer's process. However, in this case, the condensate cannot be used as boiler feed water. As previously discussed, MVR can only handle narrow areas around the design point, which limits operational flexibility.

Note that de-superheating of the steam with condensate is required to reduce the energy in the steam which can overheat the mechanical component of the compression system. However, the final enthalpy is still higher than the inlet vapor mixture.

One of the main challenges was building the MVR system to handle the flexibility of the plant, due to different products during different seasons that goes through the plant, variable capacity, and aroma recovery with/without the stripping column.

## Environmental impact

Reduction in steam requirements from 5.8 tph to 1.7 tph to handle a load of 30 tph of fruit solids. This means that specific steam consumption reduced from 0.193kg/kg to 0.057 kg/kg. However, electrciity consumption increased from 56kW to 590kW. However, the required cooling tower capacity reduced from 6700 MJ/h to 250 MJ/h as most of the waste heat was being recycled into the evaporation process.

## Current technology advancement

MVR evaporators of fruit juice has been around since the start of the 1980s, carried out with direct radial compressors that can achieve a maximum dT of $8.5 \degree C$. This is not sufficient for the final concentration required.

- Traditionally, there might be a multi-stage evaporator and TVR used to handle the final concentration/evaporation process.

- Extreme temperature change can destroy the aroma within the condensate. Therefore, a stripping column is used to remove the aroma with steam before evaporation of the aroma removed, slightly preconcentrated fruit solid.

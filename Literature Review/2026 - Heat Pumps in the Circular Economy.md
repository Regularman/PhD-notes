### Take back systems

Operational set of processes that includes the collection of EoL products, transportations, sorting, and disassembly, requalification, and re-engagement of recovered material, component of materials in the forward supply chain.
- Start small and experiment with strategies such as warranty claim, service returns and naturally occurring product recalls.
- Map out the value chain, how is the product reaching the consumer (direct sale, through retail, as a component in a larger system, or through distributors)
- To build a ribust business case, mapp all activities involved and identify the cost drivers and potential value for end users and manufacturers. Also how can partnerships be built to share costs and benefits.

You need to use data about reason for return, condition, mileage, and product type is needed to support efficient sorting and decision making.
- The success of takeback systems are dependent on the willingness of the OEM to pay for recycled components (if they have the infrastructure to do so), and driven by the cost of transportation.
###### Swiss example (vRb)

A prepaid recycling fee is built into the product cost to finance the costs of collection, transportation, and recycling of heat pumps, as well as the monitoring of the recycling company.
Disposed appliances can be handed in at partnered recycling points (SENS recycling, which has over 750 locations), or they will go around to collect the devices.
- Maximum output is 350k, recycling carried out by certified companies 
- Refrigerant have to be removed before breaking the material down into aluminum, iron, copper, and plastics.
##### Danfoss

Part of the IEA project 65. 2025

They did a pilot project to demonstrate resource efficiency and support circular business models. Currently looking into the recycling and reuse in the heat pump sector.
- This pilot case studies may provide insight into the technical and economic challenges of recycling heat pumps

Value chain requires collaboration between wholesalers, installers, and Danfoss to recycle (plastics) radiator thermostats.
- This is important for heat pumps, which also have multiple value chains, unless HaaS is implemented.
- Regulatory challenges for moving EoL products across borders.

In this case, the recalled products go straight back to Danfoss rather than a different recycling partner. This is done through a reverse logistic process

The parts are pre-sorted and disassembled to recover ABS plastic and using that to manufacture prototype thermostats to demonstrate closed loop recycling.
- The main goal is to demonstrate alignment with sustainability goals, and it does not show the economic business case.
- Recycling reduced carbon footprints by 23%, 13% by recycling ABS plastic alone.
- Uses RE:CIRC to track the circularity and sustainability of products
##### Grundfos

Established in 2021.

Partners with industrial wholesalers and installers to collect circulation pumps from users in a reverse logistic chain. Installer takes the pump to the wholesaler, who then takes it to Grundfos. Each component is then categorised into its end of life use case/bucket.

Some challenges that Grundfos has faced is 
- ensuring the traceability and quality of the returned parts
- scaling of the process to achieve economic viability

Remanufactured rotors save up to 50% of CO2 emissions versus new production. But higher return volumes are needed for the business case to mature.

- 2022, Grundfos provided NIBE (heat pump manufacturer) with 10,000 circulator pumps, all of with remanufactured **rotors.**
- NIBE stresses the importance of traceability and reliable material data to meet growing customer and regulatory demand.
##### Erion

When heat pump reach their EoL they are categorised as Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment, categorised under Directive 2012/19/EU and Leglislative Decree 49/2014 since 2018 in Europe.

- In 2024, Erion managed 76,925 tons of R1 from which it was possible to obtain the following Secondary Raw Materials: 2,231 tons of aluminum, 538 tons of other metals, 1,462 tons of copper, and 45,615 tons of iron.
- It was also possible to recover the refridgerant contained in the WEEE. In about 500-1000 tonnes of WEEE, a plant can recover 10 tons of refridgerant annually, 5 ton of which can be reclaimed into virgin refridgerant. (R410A, R470C, R134A, and R32 gases were recovered)

![[Screenshot 2026-06-11 111831.png]]

Note that installers in this program had an obligation to safely remove the refrigerant inside the appliance.
- For larger equipment, the installer can organise direct collection, where it will be taken to the correct WEEE plant for management and recycling.

Uncertified scrap yards are troublesome to the tracking and reuse of components. This is especially true for heat pumps, due to their high refrigerant, aluminium, copper, and iron content.
- Heat pumps and WEEE are intrinsically sensitive due to the high value of material, and difficulty in finding the right plant tot separate components.

Exceed Clima, funded by manufacturers that are shareholders in the Erion consortium, provide financial resources to offer simple and widespread methods to increase the quantity of WEEE collected by certified services.
- Also offers environmental training (installers may not have knowledge on how to handle the WEEE waste), manages WEEE register, and support in administrative processes.
- This includes directly calling Erion to collect the waste on site. Most people go to scrapper due to inconvenience and bureaucratic processes.
- Apply a 1-1 take back scheme such that the installer has to contact the wholesaler.
- Incentives for installers may be needed with WEEE collection, such as value sharing to shift more installers to the side of the WEEE collection flow.

This issue is extensive... Installers are assuming that the scrap market is as legally compliant.

- According to the analyses carried out for the year 2021, 148,000 tons of new equipment (air conditioners and heat pumps) have been placed on the market, of which 120,000 tons belong to the domestic segment and 28,000 tons to the professional segment. In the same year, the stock of installed units consisted of 20.7 million of domestic air conditioners and heat pumps plus 9,6 million of professional equipment. Assuming a replacement rate of 45% for domestic and 90% for professionals, we can estimate that in 2021, WEEE from air conditioning accounted for 78,800 tons (53,600 tons domestic and 25,200 tons professional). According to Erion data, 6,700 tons (6,200 from domestic and 500 from professional) were collected through the official WEEE flow, while we can assume that the remaining 72,100 tons (47,400 domestic and 24,700 professional) have been delivered to the secondary scarp market.

##### Eco-organisms in France

In France, manufacturers subscribe to an eco-organism that manages the collection and treatment of EEE.
- Eco-organisms "Ecologic" and "Ecosystem" have mutualised "repair funds" that aim to support the repair of a product out of warranty (created in 2022), to reduce the end-user cost. Repairers with the "QualRepar" label can benefit from these subsidies. 
- These eco-organism dedicate 5% of the fund they collect to reuse of EEE. 
- Concerning the heat exchange equipment (Category 1) which includes heat pumps, French regulation also sets different goals for collecting (65 % of Cat. 1 EEE), recycling (80 %) and recovering (95 %).
##### Ebm-papst

Take back system motivated by regulations such as Ecodesign Directive, The RU green Deal, and Ecodesign for Sustainable Product Regulations.
- These regulations might set certain level of heat pumps to be re-used.
- There might also be a social aspect to this motivation
- Motivated also by the lack of take-back infrastructure (which amplified economic uncertainty around recovering lower value items)

Manufactures fans, motors, and electronics for heat pumps, and in the design stage, is designing for modular construction, repair and refurbishment, and enhanced recyclability of the components.
- Furthermore, digital solutions such as condition monitoring, predictive maintenance is used to support lifecycle assessment and decision making 
	- However, this is hampered by a lack of standardised data infrastructure and design standardisation  across the supply chain.
	- Existing product lines hampers implementation of digital solution
	- Low customer incentive to participate in take back scheme
	- Fragmented international regulation prohibits cross border take backs
These issues can be addressed through stakeholder consultation and building partnerships with heat pump OEM, service partners, installers, and recyclers to establish reverse logistics and EoL collections. 



The main issue is that installers are driven by bulk material value rather than circularity strategy, and heat pumps are recycled as mixed metal scraps. There needs to be better education, convenience, and incentives to the circular economy strategy.


##### Smart pumps

Similar to Ebm-papst, smart pumps project, a part of Horizon EU, aims to develop the next stage of heat pump
- embed sensors for traceability
- redesign heat pumps to have modularity and higher efficiency
- integrate recycled materials in manufacturing processes
- Develop training modules for the workforce

### CE-RISE Viessmann project

This project involves the use of a Digital Product Passport to improve material transparency and circularity with 26 project partners

- Runs under Horizon Europe and is a 4 years EU project ending in 2026, with a budget of $9.1M euros
- This will ensure that data on the material content, recycled materials, product lifespan and its potential for repair, reuse and refurbishment and recycling is connected to a product

This will simplify compliance documentations and promote efficient reuse processes.

In this project, Viessmann contributes through its Vitocal 250-A heat pump, and the DPP tells users the mass share of steel, copper, and plastics, manufacturing origin, disassembly options, and possible pathways for reuse and recycle.
- Over a typical product lifetime of around 17 years, the total CO₂ emissions for the Vitocal 250-A heat pump are approximately 17.8 tons (17,800 kg CO₂-equivalent). Of this, about 15.3 tons come from the use phase (mainly electricity consumption), while the remainder (about 2.1 tons) arises from raw material extraction and production. Steel is the largest material contributor, with each unit containing 103 kg steel, plus smaller quantities of copper, plastics, and electronics.
- However, it does not show how the DPP is able to contribute to improving the circularity of the product
- DPP optimise the logistic to save ~131kg CO2 per unit

Lessons learnt was that a lot of suppliers (for more complex equipment such as circuit boards), cannot provide information which limits traceability and potential for recycling.
- DPP enable more components to be recovered from EOL heat pumps for repair or recycling and provides more concrete information on environmental gains

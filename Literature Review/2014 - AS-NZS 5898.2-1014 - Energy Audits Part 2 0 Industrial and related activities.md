The standard set out the process for organisations and auditors to follow when undertaking energy audits to identify opportunities for cost effective investments to improve energy performance.

There are $3$ defined types of energy audits. this standard helps the practitioners know which method is the best.
- highlights reporting requirements

#### System scope

Energy is consumed on industrial sites for the foloowing

- Control of raw material (extracting, receiving, storage, and handling)
- Conversion processes (handling, processing, distributing)
- Finished products (receiving, storage, dispatch)
- Utility supply, waste stream, or by product treatment (waste water, gases, liquids, and solids), and disposal within or external to the site
- The utilities supporting these operations

There are three types audits

- Broad estimates - Energy savings and capital costs estimated based on facility level energy data and information from similar projects previously implemented for simple engineering calculations/estimations to a lower degree of accuracy.
- Medium accuracy - Energy savings quantified using site and energy analysis, including site-specific data, operations, and other relevant context. Capital costs are estimated based on a summation of major cost items based on the configuration of systems and equipment necessary to implement the energy savings measure
- High accuracy - Including the consideration of site specific data, operations and other relevant context, with consideration of the detailed energy balance developed using the additional monitoring data. Capital costs estimated from contractor quotes and/or detailed cost estimates based on specific equipment selections and schematic designs.

## Economic criteria for energy audits 

From Appendix C2 of the standard

- Annual energy cost savings and maintenance cost savings
- Annual demand charge savings (which is the additional charge for peak demand periods)
- Payback expressed in months (Note that simple payback is a bare minimum within the standard). Therefore, energy savings and peak demand savings are also important.
- Any financial metric as agreed upon by the factory

Noting that this is dependent on energy tariffs and GST used for financial calculations. 

As per standard, detailed economic breakdown needs to be given for annual end uses greater than $20\%$ of the energy costs in type 1, and greater than $10\%$ in type 1 and type 2.

Notably, the benefits from an energy audit are

- reduced expenditure on energy
- reduced maintenance costs
- savings in other costs such as water charges or greenhouse emissions costs (carbon tax)
- Reduced capital expenditure

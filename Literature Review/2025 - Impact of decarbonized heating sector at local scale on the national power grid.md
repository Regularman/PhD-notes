## Contributions

Understand the competition mechanics between various heat production strategies, including individual and collective means, as well as the integration of thermal energy storage.
- Analysed from a CAPEX and GWP perspective

Investigating the effects of heating electrification on the national power system: what are the impacts on the load and the potential power production fleet evolution.

Most importantly, it maps local/district heating strategies to national outcomes.
- Performed mono-objective optimisation of economic and emission metrics.
- Economic metrics is just the total annual cost without any discount rate
- GWP is measured for both construction and utilisation

Trade off is then optimised through a carbon dioxide penalty through a Pareto frontier.

## Content

The paper refers to the importance of metrics to abstract the HPN, focussing on CAPEX, OPEX, and carbon emissions.
- Electricity system reliability is not assessed, as the power system is constrained such that power consumption is equal to production at all times.

Compared 5 different scenarios (all of which are greenfield),

One of the outcomes that shows the importance of metrics in designing and comparing heat production networks, the economic and environmental investment cost of a radiator is lower than that of a heat pump. But the performance of the heat pump is higher due to higher COP.
- However data uncertainty can complicate the decision to implement radiators.

Ultimately showed that integration of renewables can bring down both emissions and cost factors based on the comparison of technology implemented at the construction and operation phase.
- Most importantly analysis showed that electrification of heat supply does not require additional instalment of gas fired power plants even in winter loads, as TES is sufficient to manage peak loads. However, analysis is done in terms of GWP and costs instead of any power system relevant metrics.
- Does not address how the installation of grid connected heat supply can reduce renewable curtailment
## Limitations

However, the study only looks at heating demands.
- CHP plants can provide both power and heat and provides a valuable source of flexibility to the power system
- System level directional papers lacks the details to truly inform implementations of HPNs.
	- Technology groups are aggregated; temperature levels in district heating networks are not modelled explicitly; temperature-dependent performance, building thermal inertia, and network losses are not represented. Consequently, the results capture directions but not operational details.

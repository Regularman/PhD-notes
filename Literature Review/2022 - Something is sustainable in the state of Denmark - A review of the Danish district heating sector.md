### History of Danish District Heating

| Primary fuel type                                          | Time                                      | Pressure causing innovation                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Waste to Heat                                              | Initially started in 1903, popularised in | Initially, district heating through waste to energy was enabled through Danish cooperative spirit. However, the oil rise in the 50s and 60s led to the rise of fossil fuel based technologies.                                    |
| Oil based burners                                          | 1970s                                     | District heating experienced its largest growth due to the oil boom. The oil crisis of the 1970s and import dependency, affordability. Cause Denmark to look towards more renewable means of CHP and large scale district heating |

### Why is Danish District Heating so successful?

Currently, the Danish District Heating System supplies Danish households with 2/3 of the space heating and hot water needs. The country is also a front runner in district heating innovation and low carbon heating technologies.

- 67.7% of total heat is CHP, rest is heat only.
- Centralised CHP = 33% of district heating and decentralised CHP accounts for 15.1%, while CHPs from secondary producers accounted for 19.6% (waste treatment plants, industrial production sites, and nurseries.)

Split into co-operative district heating (34%, typically in rural regions) and centralised district heating. 
Main fuel supplies are
- Natural gas, biomass, household and other waste, waste heat, coal (CHP only), oil, thermal solar collectors, electricity and wind power (heat pumps), biogas and biomass.

Market share for district heating in buildings is 56%

Price of District heating is 97 euros/MWh. This is roughly the same across all heat density regions, and there is an effect of subsidization for lower heat density regions (which has higher heat losses and reduced efficiencies)
- Furthermore, successful district heating enabled by long term, low interest loans offered by the Municipal Credit Bank, as well as mandatory connections to firm up revenue for district heating companies
- However, the mandatory connection was recently relaxed

The other biggest district heating networks are Germany, Sweden, and China. This is where Denmark exports most of their technologies to.

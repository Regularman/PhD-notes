The AER uses a variety of methods for assessing the capex and opex forecast

- Benchmarking (efficiency)
	- Multilateral total factor productivity
	- Data envelopment analysis
	- econometric modelling
- Methodology review
- Governance and policy review
- Predictive modelling
- Trend analysis
- Cost benefit analysis
- Detailed project Review

## Benchmarking

This is driven by benchmarking across these categories
- Total capex and total opex
- Drivers of expenditure such as customer driven capex (capex to meet new customer demand and is reimbursed directly by a customer) or maintenance opex.
- Lower level benchmarking may be with unit cost and unit volume associated with a given work.

## Cost benefit analysis

Compared with other projects, in NPV terms

## Capex expenditure assessment methodology

The efficiency of expenditure is assessed through capex productivity.

- The TNSP need to submit economic analysis demonstrating that forecast expenditure is prudent and efficient
- Include all replacement capex, which is closely related to maintenance opex
- Augmentation capex, which is the need to build or upgrade the network assets to address changes in demand and to maintain quality, reliability and security of supply as specified by legislated requirements.

## Demand forecasts

Demand forecasts are important in assessing the usage rate and efficiency of the investment. They must include
- raw (historical demand peaks)
- weather corrected peak demand with different probability of exceedance (10 and 50 PoE)
- coincident peak demand
- non-coincident peak demand
- power factors
- coincidence factors

Demand forecasts may be built on economic indicators and forecasts and temperature and weather data and these must be input into the assessment methodology.

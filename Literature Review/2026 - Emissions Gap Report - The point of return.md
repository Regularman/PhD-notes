The lowest possible emission path is the $1.5\degree C$ overshoot scenario in which peak warming is limited to $1.7-1.8 \degree C$ by the middle of the century and $CO_2$ is increasingly removed from the atmosphere and stored to bring temperatures back down by $2100$. To get on this pathway, stringent greenhouse emission reductions would have to start this year to deliver cuts of $18\%$ by $2030$, $45\%$ by 2035 and $69\%$  per cent by $2040$, all relative to $202$5 levels. However, even full implementation of all current nationally determined contributions does not get anywhere near this level of cuts, leaving the world on track for global warming of $2.6°C$ by $2100$.

- By setting the lower bound to $1.5\degree C$, the UNEP is acknowledging that we can no longer achieve a multi-decadal growth of $1.5\degree C$ by $2100$.

## Trends in carbon emissions

Global emissions reached $58.4 Gt_{CO_2e}$ in $2025$ ($\pm 5.5Gt$). This represents a decline of $0.1Gt$ form 2024 levels. However, $CO_2$ emissions from fossil fuel combustion, NOx emissions, and F-gas emissions continues to rise.
- The main offsets were achieved through changes in land use, which in turn is driven by reduction in biomass burning emissions and a decline in global wildfire carbon emissions.
- Expected emissions in 2030 based on current policies are about $2-4 Gt_{CO_2e}$ above the levels implied by unconditional and conditional NDC scenarios respectively.

The main take away is that most nations needs to accelerate their emission reductions beyond current policy trends and NDC commitments to achieve long term net zero goals.
- The new 2035 NDCs strengthen the 2030 targets for most countries with the exception of Argentina, Saudi Arabia and the US (who has pulled out)

#### Sectoral goals to decarbonisation

Tripling of renewable energy capacity and doubling of the annual rate of energy efficiency improvements by 2030.

One of the main observations is that there has been significant buildout of renewables. Global renewable electricity capacity grew $3$ times faster than current policy scenarios, with solar PV buildouts increasing $7$ times fasters. The tripling of the share of wind and solar in electricity generation will be achieved by $2030$.
- However, recent rates of change across all indicators tracking absolute GHG emissions, including CO2 emissions in energy systems, road transport emissions and non-CO2 emissions, notably methane emissions, are heading in the opposite direction to the global stocktake targets.
- ~={green}This shows that the adoption of zero-low carbon technologies will not be sufficient to achieve the global stocktake outcome.=~

#### Importance of climate policies 

Studies focus on pricing, leaving the most widely used policy instruments globally including regulation, standards, public investment and subsidies, largely unassessed in the academic literature.

Based on available studies, the introduction of economic instruments, including carbon cap-and-trade schemes and carbon taxes, the instruments most frequently evaluated, have resulted in average emission reductions of 5–13 per cent across sectors over the evaluation period relative to a no-policy baseline.

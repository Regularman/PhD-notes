## Flameless oxyfuel combustion

Technology developed where combustion occurs under diluted oxygen conditions (diluted with flue gas), to reduce flame temperature below the temperature required for the formation of NOX. This also converts the flue gas to higher concentrations of hydrogen, which is more useful for carbon capture utilisation and sequestration.
- Reported energy savings of up to 65% and carbon emission savings of 20-50%.
- Technology is also applied to aluminium and copper melting furnaces
- The technology ensure even temperature distribution due to the mixing of the fuel, which reduces heating time to around 50% to that of conventional operations
Can also be potentially applied to blast furnace hot stoves, although this has not been tested yet.

## Reheating operation

Reheating of steel for rolling and annealing operations normally take 1.2-1.6GJ./t-CS. However, hot charging of crude steel into hot rolling operations can avoid this entirely. The consumption of energy on this operation in Australia is entirely dependent on whether the crude steel is transport offsite or processed in the manufacturing centre. Currently, reheating furnaces are using natural gas, LPG, coke oven gas, blast furnace gas, for reheating operations

### 2018 - Lab testing

Evaluation of hydrogen with up to 1.0MW in scale for hydrogen combustion. Showing good temperature uniformity and lower NOX emissions.
- Pilot tests were carried out on 10kg samples from 4 steel producers
- Displayed no negative impacts on the steel grade

### 2020 - Demonstration project

Collaboration with Ovako to test melting of 25 tonne of bearing steel ingot, which were successfully rolled into bar at the rolling mill

### Full scale implementation

The site at Ovako now operates a 20MW electrolyser, producing 3,880 $m^3$ of hydrogen per hour, with the potential to reduce the site emission by 20ktpa, which represents 44.6% of it carbon emissions.
- Note that the Ovako plant has a capacity of 500ktpa [Source](https://www.gem.wiki/Ovako_Hofors_steel_plant). Meaning that it will use around 800,000GJ of energy. Using an emission intensity of 56kt/PJ for natural gas, this represents 44.8kt of emissions per year in terms of scope 1 emissions.
- Sweden’s Energy Agency provided financial support through its Industrial Decarbonization initiative, backed by the EU’s Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF), part of the NextGeneration EU program aimed at promoting a more sustainable Europe.
